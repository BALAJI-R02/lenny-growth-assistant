# SKILL: Ship 30 for 30 Essay Writer

## Purpose
Generate publication-ready ~1,250-word essays following Ship 30 for 30 digital writing principles.
Essays must be grounded in Lenny's Podcast transcript evidence with inline citations.

---

## Core Ship 30 for 30 Principles

### 1. The Mindset: Digital Writer, Not Legacy Writer
Digital Writers treat writing as a startup: make small bets, gather data, iterate.
They Practice In Public — publish daily, gather rapid feedback, double down on what works.
They are entrepreneurs, not hermits waiting for inspiration.

### 2. The 5 Principles of Great Online Writing

**A. Specificity Over Generality**
The more specific your essay, the more it resonates. "How retention works" < "How Duolingo achieved 47% D30 retention using streak mechanics." Choose ONE tight idea.

**B. The Strong Hook (First 5 Lines)**
Readers decide in 3 seconds. Open with:
- A counterintuitive claim
- A provocative question
- A shocking statistic
- A personal failure/revelation
- A contrarian take on conventional wisdom
The hook should make it impossible NOT to read the next line.

**C. One Core Idea, Fully Developed**
Every essay has ONE main insight. Not five. Everything else (examples, frameworks, data) serves that single idea. If you can't say your essay's thesis in one sentence, it's not ready.

**D. Clear Narrative Progression**
Structure: Hook → Problem/Context → Core Insight → Evidence/Examples → Specific Actionable Takeaway
Each section leads inevitably to the next. The reader is always moving forward.

**E. Skimmability (The 3-Second Test)**
Assume readers will skim first. Design for it:
- Bold the 3-5 most important sentences
- Use subheadings every 200-300 words
- Use short bullet lists for lists of 3+ items
- Keep paragraphs to 3-5 lines max
- A skimmer should understand the whole essay from bold + headings alone

### 3. The Endless Idea Generator (for Essay Topics)
Topic → Credibility → 4A Paths → Proven Approach:
- **Actionable:** Here's how to do X
- **Analytical:** Here's the data on X
- **Aspirational:** You can achieve X
- **Anthropological:** Here's why X happens

### 4. Word Count Target
**~1,250 words.** Range: 1,150-1,350. Not a blog post (too long). Not a tweet thread (too short). The "atomic essay" format that works best on Substack, Medium, LinkedIn.

### 5. The Specific Useful Takeaway
End with ONE concrete thing the reader can do TODAY. Not "think about retention" but "map your activation funnel and identify the one moment that correlates with 30-day retention in your data."

## Essay Structure Template

```
TITLE: [Punchy, specific, SEO-friendly]

HOOK (100-150 words):
[Counterintuitive opener that challenges a common belief OR states a surprising insight]
[Make the problem/tension visceral]

CONTEXT/PROBLEM (150-200 words):
[Why does this matter now? What's the conventional wisdom that's wrong?]
[Set up the tension]

CORE INSIGHT (200-250 words):
[The main idea, stated clearly]
[One specific framework or mental model]
[Subheading: clear label for this section]

EVIDENCE FROM TRANSCRIPTS (300-400 words):
[3-5 specific examples from Lenny's podcast with citations [N]]
[Include specific numbers, company names, guest names]
[Subheadings per example or cluster of evidence]

IMPLICATIONS (150-200 words):
[So what? What does this mean for product builders today?]
[Connect back to the hook's tension]

ACTIONABLE TAKEAWAY (100-150 words):
[Subheading: "What To Do This Week" or similar]
[ONE specific, implementable action]
[Not general advice — make it operational]
```

---

## Quality Checklist

Before returning the essay, verify:
- [ ] Hook is in the first 5 lines and creates genuine curiosity
- [ ] ONE core idea runs through the entire piece
- [ ] At least 3 inline citations from retrieved transcript evidence [1], [2], [3]
- [ ] At least 2 subheadings (##)
- [ ] At least 1 bulleted or numbered list
- [ ] At least 5 instances of **selective bold** on key phrases
- [ ] Ends with a specific, actionable takeaway
- [ ] Word count between 1,150-1,350

---

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

❌ Generic advice ("focus on the user experience")
❌ Lists of 10+ items with no depth
❌ Starting with "In today's digital landscape..."
❌ Hedging ("this might work for some teams")
❌ Ending with "I hope this was helpful"
❌ Citing sources not in the retrieved evidence
❌ Writing about multiple disconnected ideas
